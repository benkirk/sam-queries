## What stands out

- **All 14 closed within 28 minutes of each other.** Every deactivation falls
  between `2026-07-16 23:53:42` and `2026-07-17 00:21`, inside the same
  overnight sweep that produced the 12 already-repaired users. No other
  deactivations that week share this shape.

- **`mtessler` charged 50,775 core-hours across 9 days in July** — more than
  the other 13 combined — and was deactivated 4 days after her last job, while
  leading `ULSU0003`. If that is a genuine offboarding it is an unusually
  abrupt one.

- **Five were deactivated within a day of their last job** — `asampath` (same
  day), `emeeker`, `cmorris`, `asticker`, `kboden` (1 day). People wrapping up
  do not normally run production jobs the day they leave.

- **`rhallmark` has one charge record dated 2026-07-20**, three days *after*
  her deactivation — but with **zero charges**, so this is almost certainly a
  job already queued that ran and cost nothing, not evidence of retained
  access. Flagged for completeness, not as a smoking gun.

- **4 of the 14 are the Lead of an active project**, so those projects

## What would help from the upstream side

For each row, the question is simply: **is this person actually gone?**

- If **yes** — no action. The deactivation was correct and the membership
  closure is a correct consequence.
- If **no** — they were closed in error and will need both reactivation *and*
  a membership repair, because reopening the account does not restore
  memberships (that is issue #3). Follow
  [`RUNBOOK-missing-projects.md`](RUNBOOK-missing-projects.md) after reopening.

`UPID` and `UID` are included so rows can be matched against IdMS/IGA records
directly.

## Reproducing this list

The query is the same detector as
[`2026-07-restore-orphaned-memberships.sql`](2026-07-restore-orphaned-memberships.sql)
with rule 2 inverted — `users.active = 0` instead of `= 1` — joined to July
charge activity. Re-run it to see who has since been reactivated: anyone who
drops off this list should be checked against the repair detector, because
reactivation is exactly what moves them into the broken state.
