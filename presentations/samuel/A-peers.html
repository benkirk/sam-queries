hpc-usage-queries: two toolkits, one wheel

  • “User-facing tools for analyzing resource usage on NSF NCAR HPC systems.”
  • job_history: PBS job history and charging, one database per machine.
  • fs_scans: filesystem usage, from the metadata scans of Campaign and Destor.
  • Useful without SAM: jobhist runs on the HPC hosts against SQLite or Postgres.
  • About 34K lines of Python and 930 tests, public on GitHub.

†Same CLI shape as SAMuel’s on purpose: commands, exit codes, JSON output. The two change together.

github.com/benkirk/hpc-usage-queries. Both toolkits ship in one wheel; SAMuel installs it through its [hpc] extra and loads job_history and fs_scans as two plugins (sam/plugins.py). Shared conventions: the Context/BaseCommand hierarchy, EXIT_* codes, the JSON envelope with kind=. jobhist also has an exporter registry (dat, csv, md, json files); SAMuel’s CLIs print rich or json only. CLAUDE.md in both repos says to change the shared parts in lockstep.

job_history: logs to charges

  • One charge row per job, priced by this repo’s rules.
  • Whole nodes on Derecho’s production queues; actual CPUs and GPUs on the dev queues.
  • QoS factors: premium 1.5, regular 1.0, economy 0.7.
  • SAMuel reads the daily summaries, not the jobs, to charge allocations.

job_history/sync/charging.py (DerechoCharging, CasperCharging). A database trigger keeps exactly one job_charges row per job; daily_summary is keyed by date, user, account and queue, cut at Mountain midnight. pbsparse (NCAR, public) does the parsing, plus a vendored NCAR record class that adds power metrics. sam-admin accounting –comp calls JobQueries.daily_summary_report() and upserts comp_charge_summary. The sync runs from SAMuel’s ncar-hpc-deploy lane; the schedule is Part 4’s, the rules are this repo’s.

fs_scans, and the cluster it brings

fs_scans

  • Weekly metadata scans of GPFS, Lustre and POSIX filesystems.
  • Imported into campaign and destor, one schema per collection.
  • SAMuel’s disk-scan views read them through the replica.

csg-postgres

  • The CNPG chart lives in this repo, not SAMuel’s.
  • Three roles: owner, jobhist_writer, read-only.
  • A read-only service on the replica, and a watch script.

fs_scans/parsers/{gpfs,lustre,posix}.py and a three-pass importer; the scan PBS jobs live in fs_scans/PBS/. helm/ is the cirrus-csg-postgres chart (2 instances, secrets from OpenBao); pg_service_ro.yaml is csg-postgres-ro.k8s.ucar.edu; scripts/cnpg_watch.sh. SAMuel calls require_plugin(): a missing package turns the jobs pages, My Jobs, disk scans and sam-search accounting –jobs off, rather than failing.

hpc-scheduling-tools: SAM to PBS, kept apart

  • “These tools sit between two production systems and translate one into the other.”
  • The fairshare tree (fsparsetree_mr): PBS’s resource_group file. This repo owns it.
  • The hooks and their database (samuel2sql): HSG’s. This repo keeps copies to test against and iterate on.
  • Read-only against SAM, through three legacy-shaped endpoints: fstree, queues, wallclock exemptions.
  • Casper, Derecho and Gust. A private NCAR repo.

†HSG runs and maintains their own hooks and samuel2sql; ours have moved on from theirs.

github.com/NCAR/hpc-scheduling-tools (private). The accounting hook rejects a bad -A account at submit; the wallclock hook applies exemptions, then queue limits. The deployed hooks live in NCAR/PBS_hooks (private, branch production), and HSG maintains their own samuel2sql; this repo’s sample_hooks/ and samuel2sql are reference copies Ben has iterated on since (Ben, 2026-10-02). The database layout is a contract (docs/PBS_HOOKS.md). capacities.json is the operator-tuned input.

Packaged once, served by the API

  • The scheduling rules live in their own repo, tested there, away from SAMuel’s.
  • SAMuel installs it as a plugin and runs fsparsetree_mr inside the API: /api/v1/fairshare/<machine>.
  • HSG’s cron downloads the tree and installs nothing on the scheduler hosts.
  • The next artifact can follow the same path.

The [hpc] extra installs it from git; the HPC_SCHEDULING_TOOLS plugin backs src/webapp/api/v1/fairshare.py, which reads SAM’s database directly (no HTTP round trip) and returns JSON or ?format=text. 503 unless HPC_SCHEDULING_TOOLS_ENABLED. ncar_accounting.db is not served by the API: HSG builds it with their own samuel2sql from the legacy-shaped endpoints. Same story as Part 4’s “SAMuel to PBS, in one slide”.

Legacy SAM: the original

  • 221K lines of Java: Spring, Hibernate and a JSF/PrimeFaces UI, built into one WAR on Tomcat.
  • Two surfaces: the web UI, and the REST APIs that SAMuel has been porting.
  • On the sam-tomcat VM, sharing sam-sql with SAMuel.
  • In service for nearly 15 years. A private NCAR repo.

†Part 4 has how the two apps share the database, and who still calls legacy.

github.com/NCAR/sam (private): about 2,900 .java files and 229 .xhtml pages, Maven-built. Part 4: “Two apps, one database” and “Who’s still on legacy”.

TL;DR - peer projects

  • hpc-usage-queries: job history, charging rules and filesystem scans; two plugins and the Postgres cluster.
  • hpc-scheduling-tools: the fairshare tree, kept in its own repo and served by our API; the hooks stay HSG’s.
  • Legacy SAM: the Java original, sharing our database until it retires.
  • The container zoo: identity and AMIE helpers; syncd is the one still in daily use.
  • Retired or retiring: one list, shrinking.
